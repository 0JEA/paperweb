Edges, folds and crumple

Every sheet below is the same flat stock with one layer varied, so the differences are the parameter and nothing else. Seeds differ per sheet, which is why no two are the same piece of paper.

Edge

The silhouette is one signed distance field with the displacement sampled at the nearest point on the boundary, so it stays continuous through the corners. The cast shadow is thrown by the sheet's body, not by its fibres, which is what stops a torn edge reading as a row of black teeth.

Folds

count is a maximum and each candidate has to pass chance, so sheets genuinely differ in how used they look. Creases are biased toward horizontal, turn vertical near the left and right edges, and do not always run the full width.

Crumple

Each Worley cell is a tilted plane faded to zero at the boundary it shares with its neighbour, so the sheet stays continuous and the creases are angle changes rather than cliffs. crease blends back toward a sharp crack network; above about 0.05 it starts to read as dried mud rather than paper.
