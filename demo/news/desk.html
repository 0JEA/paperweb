paperweb showcase

The Newsroom Desk

Twenty editorial components, each built as the physical thing an editor actually kept on the desk: pinned, spiked, clipped, stacked, weighted down and scribbled on. Every cream surface below is a live WebGL2 paper render. The pins, clips, spikes, bands and coffee rings are CSS and inline SVG.

Idea. Killed copy, impaled. Two badly-handled sheets skewered on a desk spindle drawn in SVG; the shaft sits behind the paper, the needle tip in front, and each sheet carries a small torn pierce hole where the steel went through. preset worn · dpi 120 · folds {count 2, depth 0.9} · crumple {amplitude_um 60, crease 0.18} · edge.deckle_px 7–9 Crumple is a facet model, so crease stays low: it blends toward a sharp Voronoi ridge network, and past about 0.2 the sheet reads as dried mud rather than as paper someone screwed up.

Correction notice · desk copy

Second carbon · desk

Correction

An earlier edition reported that the parking levy passed eight votes to three. The vote was seven to four; Councillor Reyes abstained and was recorded in error. The Ledger regrets the mistake and has corrected the record online.

Filed 18:12 · corrections@ledger

Idea. A carbon flimsy: thin, faintly translucent, the type violet-grey and pressed into the sheet rather than printed on it. This is the rasterize showpiece: the text is snapshotted and run through Kubelka-Munk, so it takes the paper's shading. content: 'rasterize' · tone.opacity 0.86 · ink.ink_over_white [0.16,0.14,0.22] · ink.granulation 0.5 Note: rasterized content loses interactivity, and CSS transform and column-count do not survive the foreignObject snapshot, so lay these out with flex.

06

Memo pad, top sheet torn

Breaking alert

Ledger news desk · pad 7

Breaking · 14:02

Council freezes the parking levy

Seven to four, after two hours. Reyes abstained. Full vote breakdown to follow. Halloran is in the chamber.

Idea. A breaking alert as the top sheet of a memo pad, half ripped off the glue strip. Two surfaces: the pad (aged, coarse, heavy shadow) and the sheet on top of it, torn along a fibrous deckle edge with a single fold where somebody's thumb bent it. top: preset worn · dpi 128 · edge.deckle_px 9 · folds {count 1, depth 0.6, sharpness 0.5} · pad: dpi 110 · tone.paper [0.96,0.92,0.84] Worth knowing: a fold is a broad tilt over about 14 mm plus a sub-millimetre crease line, and peak relief is only depth × 43 µm, so a single thumb-bend wants a depth near 0.6 to be visible at all. sharpness sets the crease width, not the fold's. The sheet keeps worn's own spec_intensity: the highlight shoulder holds the sheen short of white, so there is nothing left to damp.

Front page on the blotter

Lead story · the page itself

Founded 1897 · City Edition · Three Star

THE LEDGER

TuesdayWater & WorksPrice 60c

The city signed before the ink was dry

A twelve-million-dollar water main contract went to a company incorporated three weeks before the bid closed. Nobody at City Hall can say who read the tender.

By M. Halloran
The tender for the Ninth Street water main closed on a Friday afternoon in March. By the following Tuesday the work had been awarded, and the company that won it had existed for nineteen days.

Its registered address is a mailbox above a dry cleaner. Its sole director shares a surname with a former assistant in the procurement office, who left the city's employ in January and has not returned four calls.

The city says the process was competitive. Asked how many bids were received, three departments gave three different numbers, and the only written record anyone could produce was a one-page summary. cont. p.4

Idea. The lead story is the whole page, weighted onto a green leather desk blotter with four corner straps. Low DPI is the trick here: at dpi 76 the cockle buckling scales up to something a broadsheet-sized sheet would actually do, where the default 96 would look like a postcard blown up. light: 'pointer' rakes the light across it as the cursor moves; only two of twelve passes re-run. preset reading · dpi 76 · cockle.wavelength_mm 34 · light pointer

Idea. A live blog as continuous-form wire copy, tractor-feed holes down both sides and perforations across the fanfold. Very low DPI plus three sharp folds is what sells the concertina: at dpi 70 the fold creases land at printer-paper scale instead of stationery scale. The sprocket holes are a repeating radial-gradient painted the colour of the desk, so they read as punched through. preset paper · dpi 70 · folds {count 3, depth 1.4, sharpness 0.8} A fanfold crease is the deepest thing on this page, so the depth is well past 1: peak relief is depth × 43 µm, and sharpness 0.8 pulls the crease line down to about 0.7 mm so it stays a line rather than a band.

Idea. The chart is half unrolled and taped down at both top corners; the rest is still a tube with two rubber bands round it. Letters to the editor

Letters · 40 received, two printed

“Nobody asked us about the mural”

Sir, I have lived on Bell Street for thirty-one years and I found out that the mural was being painted over from the woman who sells me my newspaper. If the city consulted anybody, it was not anybody on Bell Street.

R. Okonkwo, Bell Street

“Nineteen days is not a company”

Sir, your reporter asked the right question and got four different answers, which is itself the answer. Print the one-page summary. Let us all read it together.

T. Vasquez, by email

Idea. Letters as the sheet that sat under somebody's mug all afternoon. The rings are two radial-gradient ellipses at mix-blend-mode: multiply, and the paper's own relief shows through the stain, which is the entire reason not to draw stains as flat blobs. The imperfect layer adds the sparse pits and blotches underneath, at half the density it looks like it should want: pits and marks are scanned across the 3×3 neighbourhood and render whole, so a given density reads about twice as busy as the number suggests. preset worn · dpi 105 · imperfect {pit_density 0.01, mark_density 0.025} · crumple {amplitude_um 30, crease 0.05} · folds.depth 0.8 · edge.deckle_px 6
