02

Daylight & lamplight

Dark mode for paper is not "invert the page". A sheet has no black state. The honest answer is a dimmer, warmer sheet under a lower, harder lamp: tone.paper scaled to 30% and pushed warm, light.altitude_deg 30, spec_intensity 0.9, relief exaggerated harder so the grain survives the loss of luminance, and the ink inverted in CSS. The toggle in the masthead calls paper.set() on all 30 surfaces at once. These two swatches are pinned to their own stock so the comparison holds in either mode.

Daylight stock

#FFF3DE at full luminance

The default sheet. Warm highlights, cool shadows, duotone 0.5. Comfortable in a lit room and brutal at 1 a.m.

Lamplight stock

Same sheet, 30% of the light

Not a dark rectangle: still paper, still buckled, lit from lower down so the cockle reads by raking light instead of by brightness.

      The crossing, part three

      The bridge the city has been not-building for forty years

      “Eleven words have moved this file further than four decades of consultation.”Councillor D. Reyes, in the chamber

      The first drawings were filed in 1984, on paper this colour, by an engineer who has since died twice over in the record: once in the register, once in the footnotes of a report that got the span wrong by nine metres and was never corrected.

      Every council since has voted on some version of it. Four have voted yes. None of the four had the money at the same time as the votes, which is the whole story of the crossing and, most weeks, the whole story of the city.

      What changed this spring was not the engineering. It was a federal transfer with a clause in it: spend by 2029, or send it back. The clause is eleven words long and it has done more to move the file than four decades of consultation.

      The chamber knows this. That is why Monday's vote, procedurally a formality about a feasibility envelope, drew the largest public gallery since the arena.

      Related stories rail

      A scroll-snap rail whose cards are papered with overhang:'inset', because a 'grow' canvas is clipped by the scroller and drags a horizontal scrollbar in with it. Inset shrinks the sheet and pads the element instead, which is the correct trade inside any overflow container.
