Marginalia, three hands

Pencil, ballpoint, editor's red, plus the rust the paperclip left

Reading file · annotated copy · do not re-file

What the paper knew, and when it knew it

Retrospective · 12 March 2026

For thirty-one years the Ledger-Chronicle reported that the cause of the 1974 fish kill was never established. That sentence appeared in eleven separate stories, the last of them in 2003.

It was established. It was established on 9 March 1974, in an internal note that this newspaper had in its possession by the following week. The note recommended that the results not be released until the works had been given an opportunity to comment. No such comment was ever printed, and neither were the results.

What we have been able to reconstruct is not a conspiracy so much as a filing decision: the note went into folder 3, folder 3 went into the morgue, and the story went on being written from cuttings.

The Ledger-Chronicle
Tuesday 12 March 1974 · City edition · Ten cents

Cedar Reservoir Closed After Fish Kill

Four thousand dead along the western shore. Works manager blames the weather; city engineer will not say what the samples were tested for.

The city closed Cedar Reservoir to all use on Monday after crews recovered an estimated four thousand dead fish along the western shore, most of them within a half mile of the Halloway Chemical Works outfall.

Works manager R. Pettibone said the plant had discharged nothing out of the ordinary and attributed the kill to warm weather and low water in the channel.

The city engineer's office confirmed it had taken samples on Sunday evening but declined to say what was being tested for, or when the results would be published.

Residents of the Cedar Flats district said the smell had been noticeable for a fortnight. One, who asked not to be named because her husband works at the plant, said the water had run yellow twice since Christmas.

A spokesman for the province said the reservoir would remain closed until the city was satisfied it was safe, and that no timetable had been set.

Council will meet in special session on Thursday. The works and utilities committee has asked for a written report in time for the meeting.

The reservoir has supplied the district since 1948. It was last tested in full in 1969, according to the engineer's annual report, which does not list the substances tested for.

Anglers have been asked to keep off the western shore. The medical officer of health said there was no cause for alarm and that the closure was a precaution.

Water is being trucked to the Cedar Flats standpipes twice daily. The city said the arrangement would hold for as long as it is needed.
