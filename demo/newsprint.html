Newsprint

Three effects that act on the ink rather than on the paper. All three need content to work on, so they do nothing in the default content: 'behind' mode where the text is live DOM sitting on top of the canvas. Each sheet below is fed a rendered page as its content texture.

Show-through

Type printed on the reverse of the sheet, faintly visible because newsprint is thin. The most newspaper-specific effect available and the cheapest one here: sample the content mirrored in x and let it through in proportion to how thin the stock is. It should never be readable. If you can read it, it reads as a printing fault.

0.09

Dot gain

Newsprint is absorbent, so ink wicks along the fibres and every stroke fattens. This is why newsprint type reads heavier than book type at the same size, and why newspaper faces were cut with open counters and sturdy serifs to survive it. It is a dilation, not a blur: the ink spreads outward without thinning where it came from, and the reach is uneven because the fibres are.

0.22 mm

Ink cracking on folds

Where a crease crosses solid ink, the dried film flakes off along it: the paper folds, the ink does not. Only solid ink cracks, so this scales with coverage, and it needs a crease to work with. Both sheets below are creased identically; only the flaking differs.
